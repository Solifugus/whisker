# Answers — from the RV-9 session, 2026-10-24

Replying to `docs/rv9-ask-2026-10-24.md`. Item 1 was not the panel; the
problem was in wslop's own layout, and you need to fix it in `src/module_slop.c`.
Item 2 was right and is changed.

---

## 1. The picture is where `/w0` says; wslop's marks were not

**The cause is `wsk__natural`.** A `WSK_BUTTON` with `size = 0` in a column
takes `WSK_BTN_ROWS`, which is 64 rows, whatever its area says. `wsk_rect`
clamps `extra` at 0, so the button does not shrink to fit. It runs past the
bottom of its area. In wslop that meant:

- the red edge bar, area 4 rows, was drawn **64 rows tall**. At this panel's
  pitch that is about 9.6 mm, which is "a centimetre".
- the cyan bar started at row 596 and ran 64 rows, so only rows 596–599 were on
  the glass. That is 4 rows at the very edge, and the bezel hides them.
- every mark was a **12 × 64 bar** hanging from where it was meant to be
  centred (Matthew saw "a yellow vertical bar"). Its centre sits 26 rows below
  the target. The bottom marks run off the glass at 599, which is the "top
  half" you saw.

**How it was settled.** I first moved the panel's vertical flip out of MADCTL
and into the blit. The picture came out upside down, and the red bar was still
the thick one, now at the bottom. The thickness followed the colour and not the
edge of the glass, so the fault was in what was drawn. I reverted that change.
Then I set explicit sizes in RV-9's copy, `modules/wslop/wslop.c`:
`mark[0].size = MARK - 2` and `bar[i].size = EDGE`. On the glass the red line
is now thin and the mark is a small square. Port the same two lines to
`src/module_slop.c`. The copy in RV-9's tree is still uncommitted.

**What this does to your numbers.**

- **Bias: void.** The measured +37 to +48 is about 26 rows of layout, plus
  wherever a person aims on a tall bar, plus any real touch offset. Only a
  re-run can separate those. The 45-row display offset in design 5b and your
  roadmap is not real.
- **Scatter: mostly stands,** because it is spread about the mean and a
  constant offset drops out. The bottom two marks are the exception: they were
  clipped at the edge, which squeezed the taps. Their 11–12 px is probably
  flattering.
- **Drift: stands.**

**For the design, not this bug.** A widget's natural size silently beats its
area: there is no error, and the widget paints outside the space it was given.
The cost was a day's wrong conclusion and an ask to the wrong layer. Whether
`wsk_rect` should clamp to the span, refuse, or keep it documented is your
decision. Item 1 is what not deciding cost.

## 2. The warning: agreed, reworded

You are right that it cannot tell the two states apart. A healthy GT911 that
nobody has touched answers the bus, never raises ready, and reports no finger,
which is exactly the not-scanning pattern. The driver cannot know whether
anyone is pressing. The warning now says what it sees and makes the remedy
conditional:

> the controller answers the bus and has reported no finger yet. Untouched
> glass looks the same. If it is being pressed and ready stays 0, the controller
> is not scanning; ... take the board's power away and put it back, holding the
> board rather than leaving it flat on a desk.

Holding the board matters. RV-9's design §56 found that a power cycle with the
board flat on a desk can leave the controller with a bad baseline, so taking
the power away is necessary but not always enough. Your run 1 is recorded
there as the reason for the change.
