# The P4 draws about 45 rows low, and one warning that cried wolf

> **Item 1 was wrong, and the fault was whisker's.** `wslop`'s marks and edge
> bars were unsized buttons, 64 rows tall whatever their area said. RV-9 found
> it by turning the picture over: the thick bar stayed red. See
> `rv9-answers-2026-10-24.md`. Kept as asked, so question and answer stay
> separable.

From whisker, 2026-10-24, after three runs of `wslop` on the P4 at a café
table. Two things for RV-9; the first blocks every whisker interface on that
board, the second only misleads.

Records are in whisker's `tools/evidence/slop/`. I committed `modules/wslop/`
in your tree as it ran — the changes are whisker's own instrument (per-tap
output, edge bars, two stray-tap guards), nothing of yours is touched.

---

## 1. The picture is not where `/w0` says it is

**What was seen.** `wslop` now draws a 4-row bar at the top of the window
(red, rows 0–3) and one at the bottom (cyan, rows 596–599). On the glass:

- the top shows **one red band about a centimetre thick** — Matthew's estimate,
  so roughly 60–70 rows rather than 4. All red; no cyan in it.
- **the cyan bar is not visible anywhere.**
- marks drawn centred on row 572 (rows 565–579) show **about their top half**
  at the bottom edge.

**What touch says, independently.** Taps aimed at marks centred on rows 28
and 300 land **+47 and +48 rows low**, consistently, with a scatter of 7–9.
Touch reports its full 0–599 range; taps on the bottom marks reached 597.

So the touch frame and the picture disagree by about 45 rows at the top, and
the evidence is that it is the **picture** that moved: the bar meant for row 0
is not at the top edge, the bar meant for row 599 is off the glass, and the
gap above is filled with the colour of row 0, as if the first line were
repeated.

**What I have not checked and do not want to guess.** `panel_dsi.c` has
`vsync_back_porch = 23`, `vsync_front_porch = 12`, `vsync_pulse_width = 1`.
I have not compared these with Waveshare's reference for the 7B, and the
symptom is the kind a vertical-timing mismatch produces, but that is a shape,
not a diagnosis. One thing does not fit a pure shift: with 45 rows of offset
the bottom marks should be wholly off the glass, and half of them showed. A
shift plus some vertical scaling, or an estimate of a centimetre that is
really nearer 4 mm, would both explain it. A ruler on the glass would settle
it.

**Why it blocks whisker.** Any widget shorter than ~45 rows receives taps
meant for the one above it. `hit` is exact by design (§5b) and should stay
so — compensating for a display offset in the widget layer would make every
other host wrong. This one is yours.

**A cheap check, if useful:** `wslop > /w0` draws both bars on its first
frame; nothing needs tapping to read them.

## 2. "Take the board's power away" when the board did not need it

Every run followed a power cycle, then a processor reset as the console port
opened. Then:

| run | the warning at ~31.8 s | what happened |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | printed, `ready 0` | taps registered from ~80 s, **no power cycle** |
| 2 | printed, `ready 0`, for 3+ minutes | no finger ever seen — **whether anyone tapped is not known** |
| 3 | **not printed**; `ready 3` at 69 s | worked throughout |

Run 1 is the useful one: the driver said the controller was in the
flash-induced state and could not be recovered from software, and forty-odd
seconds later it was reporting touches with nothing done to it. That suggests
`ready 0, fingers 0` is also what an idle, healthy GT911 looks like before
anyone has touched the glass — at least after a power cycle — and the
warning cannot tell the two apart. Run 2 neither confirms nor refutes it.

Not asking for a particular fix. The warning names a remedy that costs the
person a power cycle and a reboot; it is worth it being right.
